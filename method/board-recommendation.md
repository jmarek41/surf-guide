# Board recommendation method

Board selection is personal. Weight and volume are inputs, not a complete
recommendation.

## 1. Build the evidence base

Start with boards the rider has actually used:

| Board | Dimensions | Volume | Waves | Easy parts | Problems | Verdict |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|

Compare successful and unsuccessful boards for:

- volume relative to weight;
- length, width, and thickness;
- rail and foil distribution;
- rocker, tail, bottom, and fin setup;
- construction and durability;
- paddling, wave entry, stability, duck diving, and turning;
- wave type and size.

Repeated personal outcomes are stronger fit evidence than a generic volume
calculator.

## 2. Define the job

A board brief must state:

- target waves and expected power;
- ability and progression goal;
- paddle fitness and surf frequency;
- manoeuvrability versus stability;
- duck-dive requirement;
- durability and travel constraints;
- budget, local availability, trip/session duration, and whether new, used,
  rental, or any mode is acceptable.

Avoid recommending a board category without connecting its design choices to
that job.

## 3. Set a range

Use litres per kilogram only to compare boards for this rider. Do not apply one
fixed band across all abilities, constructions, shapes, and conditions.

Prefer:

- a volume range with a reasoned centre;
- dimensional and foil constraints;
- acceptable shape families;
- explicit hard exclusions;
- uncertainty when history is sparse.

How foam is distributed matters. Two boards with the same volume can differ
substantially in rails, outline, duck-dive feel, paddling, and turning.

## 4. Evaluate an available board

Capture:

- model and construction;
- length, width, thickness, and stated volume;
- tail, rails, rocker, bottom contours, and fin system where known;
- for used boards: repairs, dents, delamination, water ingress, and fin-box
  condition;
- included fins/leash/bag;
- new price, used asking price, or rental price/duration;
- location, retrieval date, and original URL.

Separate:

- shop, seller, owner, or rental-operator statements;
- manufacturer specifications;
- independent reviews;
- inference from photos.

Never infer exact volume or construction from a model name alone.

## 5. Used-board inspection

Inspect in natural light:

- sight down deck, bottom, rails, and stringer for twist, buckle, or crease;
- check nose, tail, rails, leash plug, and every fin box;
- look and press gently for delamination or soft areas;
- inspect repairs for cracks, gaps, discoloration, or fresh uncured resin;
- compare weight with a similar dry board where possible;
- ask whether open dings were surfed and how repairs were performed;
- confirm fins fit securely.

Structural uncertainty should outweigh a bargain price. When unsure, get a
repairer or experienced local surfer to inspect it.

## 6. New-board checks

- Verify the manufacturer's current dimensions, volume, construction, fin
  system, included fins, warranty, and lead time.
- Confirm that the listed size is actually stocked locally.
- Treat custom sizing as a higher-uncertainty recommendation unless the rider
  has strong history with the shape.
- Compare value against available used and rental equivalents, not just retail
  price.

## 7. Used price and market

Asking prices are not sale prices. Compare current local candidates of similar:

- model and construction;
- age and condition;
- included fins/accessories;
- demand and resale liquidity.

Do not publish a universal percentage of retail as fair value. Markets,
currencies, season, taxes, and supply vary.

## 8. Rental checks

A rental recommendation still needs board-level fit. Verify:

- exact model or dimensions, volume, construction, and fin setup;
- rental duration and total price;
- swap policy as conditions or fit change;
- damage policy, deposit, and repair charges;
- reservation certainty and pickup/return hours;
- whether fins, leash, and board protection are included.

For short trips or uncertain conditions, a flexible swap policy can be more
valuable than obtaining the theoretically perfect board for one forecast.

## 9. Buy-and-resell trips

For a board that must be sold before departure:

- prefer common, legible specifications and locally liquid shapes;
- photograph condition immediately after purchase;
- repair water-entry damage promptly;
- identify private-sale and shop/consignment channels on arrival;
- list with enough time to avoid a forced final-day sale;
- treat a shop trade-in or consignment as a fallback, not an assumed price.

Store transactions, rental bookings, and private contact details only under
`data/boards/`.

## 10. Ranking options

Rank the available choices by:

1. fit for the rider and target waves;
2. confidence in the specifications;
3. condition and structural confidence where used;
4. flexibility and availability;
5. total cost for the required duration;
6. resale considerations where ownership is temporary.

Do not let acquisition mode override a materially better fit without explaining
the compromise.

## 11. Safety and authority

The agent may research and advise. It may not contact shops, sellers, owners, or
rental operators; make offers; reserve boards; transfer money; buy; or rent
without explicit user authorization.
